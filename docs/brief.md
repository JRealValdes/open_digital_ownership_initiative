# Brief: Digital Ownership for Video Games

**Open Digital Ownership Initiative** · v1.0.0 · ~5–10 minute read  
**Audience:** platform policy / product leads, publishers, press, advocates  
**Full proposal:** [framework.md](framework.md) · **Licence:** [CC BY 4.0](../LICENSE)

---

## The one-sentence pitch

Digital distribution can remain the default—and still treat a full-price
**purchase** as **ownership** of a game copy: keep it, preserve it, lend
it, and transfer it—without requiring a permanent return to discs as the
only proof of belonging.

---

## The problem (neutral framing)

As games move to digital-first sales, many storefront purchases grant a
**non-transferable licence** tied to an account. Players often cannot
resell, lend, or reliably keep access if a store or service disappears.
Physical media had limits, but it made one fact clear: the buyer held a
copy they could keep and pass on.

The industry does not need to choose between “digital convenience” and
“real ownership.” It needs a purchase model that delivers both.

Subscriptions and rentals can coexist. This brief is about products sold
as **buy / own / purchase**.

---

## What we propose

A platform-scoped ownership model with four **non-negotiable** rights for
a purchased game copy:

| Right | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **Own** | Purchase creates ownership of that copy—not revocable storefront permission. |
| **Preserve** | Owners may back up and archive what they own so longevity is not trapped in one convenience layer. |
| **Lend** | Temporary play rights can be delegated; the owner retains ownership. |
| **Transfer** | Gift, sale, and second-hand acquisition remain possible. |

**Supporting design:**

- Ownership belongs to the **person**, not the account (accounts manage; they do not *be* ownership).
- Two modes of the same right: **Digital Ownership** (default, account-custodied) and **Offline Ownership** (unique physical ownership token—modern analogue of a disc as proof of ownership, not necessarily as the only file store).
- One **unified library**: digital and physical/offline copies live under the same ownership concept; owners can switch modes instead of buying twice.
- Multi-device use **within** a platform. Cross-ecosystem portability (e.g. Store A → rival Store B) is **out of scope** for this version.
- Security targets **ownership fraud / duplication**, not ordinary owners; permanent always-online play is not the default control.

---

## Why platforms and publishers might care

- **Trust:** Purchase-priced catalogue retains the meaning of “buy.”
- **Secondary markets with incentives:** Official digital resale can share revenue (split is an implementation choice; the right to transfer is not).
- **Physical continuity without depending on disc manufacturing alone:** Offline tokens / bridged physical editions keep retail and peer transfer meaningful in a download-first world.
- **Clear product categories:** Own vs subscribe vs rent—less regulatory and reputational risk from blurred “purchase” language.
- **Open design:** Community proposal under CC BY 4.0; critique and improvement via public RFCs—not a locked vendor pitch.

---

## What this is not

- Not a boycott campaign or a demand aimed at any single company.
- Not a ban on subscriptions, streaming catalogues, or rentals.
- Not a claim that piracy can be engineered away completely.
- Not a model that relies on voluntary honesty for “files without proof must not play”: launch must bind to a digital credential or offline token (see framework §12.3 / §13).
- Not a finished protocol, chip design, or legal statute.
- Not a requirement that one purchase work across competing platform families.
